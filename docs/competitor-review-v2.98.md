# Loadnote competitor review after v2.98

Reviewed October 5, 2026 (UTC), against Loadnote's implemented v2.98 branch. Competitor facts come from current official product/help pages; I did not sign into or perform hands-on competitor tests. Advertised adaptation and recovery features are product claims, not independent evidence of superior training outcomes. Recommendations below are product judgments based on those sources and the Loadnote implementation. Subscription prices and regional feature availability are outside this review.

Loadnote has a useful foundation for athletes combining strength, muscle growth and sport: reviewed targets, separate technical/drill evidence, explicit unknowns, recoverable drafts and traceable approvals. Its largest gaps are the effort needed to create a good plan, sustained progression after the initial approved adjustment, and a finished native product. More conversational answers alone will not close those gaps.

## Ordered roadmap

| Priority | Concrete deliverable | Acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P0: release reliability** | Signed physical-device beta; verify termination/upgrade/offline restore, background timers, keyboard/accessibility, and backup recovery. Complete production account/cloud/voice work before advertising those services in the native beta. | Actual iOS/Android device results and restore evidence; CI compilation and browser bridge mocks are separate checks. Track crash/save failures and support response. |
| **P1: guided first plan** | Three entry paths: muscle growth, Olympic lifting and athlete support. Reuse confirmed goals/equipment. Offer coach-authored templates, expose missing inputs, then show the exact week-by-week review. | In usability sessions, an athlete starts an appropriate reviewed plan without developer help. Measure time, abandonment and navigation mistakes; target under five minutes as a product goal, not a measured result. |
| **P1: continuous reviewed progression** | Repeated approved-load tracking, preserved rationale, comparable outcomes at the new dose and the next supported proposal. Include hold/defer/decline, protected weeks and changed restrictions. | Two cycles of approvals and subsequent eligible evidence work without rewriting frozen plans. Missing RPE, altered prescriptions and insufficient history withhold increases. Show association and uncertainty, not causal benefit. |
| **P1: practical session alternatives** | Reviewable responses to “I only have 30 minutes,” equipment unavailable, missed training or a practice/game change. Preserve the goal and separate technical, strength, speed and conditioning work. | Each alternative lists changed doses/dates, purpose and conflicts before approval. Chat cannot silently reschedule or apply it; unsupported changes request the needed input. |
| **P2: better exercise instruction** | Curated demos, concise cues and reviewed alternatives linked to exact exercise identities; human video feedback for technical lifting. | Content ownership and review are clear. A substitution preserves the intended domain and identifies what changed; video requires explicit access/consent. |
| **P2: activity and device integration** | Start with source-aware health/activity import; then select the highest-value native timer/watch workflow. | Duplicate activity does not inflate workload. Timestamps, units, imported versus athlete-reported measurements and unknowns survive sync. Missing wearable data never becomes a fabricated recovery score. |
| **P3: human-coach collaboration** | Minimal invited-coach review, shareable plan handoff and feedback on exact prescriptions. | Access can be revoked; coach actions are attributable; completed records remain immutable. Validate demand before building billing, teams or a public marketplace. |

## What should follow this update

**Recommended v2.99: guided plans and continuous reviewed progression.** These make the intelligence usable every week: less setup, a clear current target, relevant feedback, a supported next action and a visible reason. Develop native release acceptance alongside that work. Then prioritize specialist instruction and activity import according to observed athlete friction.

Persistent preferences should be explicit and editable: preferred equipment, feasible days, exercise alternatives and restrictions with dates/provenance. Separate preferences from temporary check-ins. Evaluate personalization using comparable histories and a baseline policy; avoid automatically learning dose rules from one successful workout.

Loadnote should retain its current separation of measured work, athlete reports and unknowns, and its deliberate approval boundary. Those are concrete implementation properties, not a claim that competing products lack safeguards. UI text can be shorter while preserving detail in the evidence view. The next research step is hands-on task benchmarking and athlete usability sessions; this source review cannot establish comparative speed, reliability or training effectiveness.
